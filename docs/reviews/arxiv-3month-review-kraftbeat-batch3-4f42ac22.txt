KRAFTBEAT / AUDIO - TOP-PAPER TAKEAWAYS, 3 AUG - 3 NOV 2026 (BATCH 3 OF THE THREE-MONTH REVIEW)
Three papers worth your time for KraftBeat. Why these: the window turned music generation
toward explicit, editable symbolic planning - which is exactly the interface a
generation app needs - plus one small audio model that fits your hardware outright.

====================================================================
1. YuE2: Unifying Symbolic and Audio Music Generation at Frontier Quality
arXiv:2609.33757 (27 Sep 2026), HKUST / M-A-P / Tokenwave / NYU / Stanford / MBZUAI / ACE Studio
Code: https://github.com/multimodal-art-projection/YuE ; weights: https://huggingface.co/m-a-p/YuE2-3B
Attention: 245 upvotes, #2 HF daily of 29 Sep
Coverage: full HTML text read (abstract, architecture, training, editing/cover sections);
evaluation tables read at claim level, not every number re-verified.

WHAT IT IS
One ~3.58B-parameter model that composes in symbols and performs in audio: an AR-NAR
Mixture-of-Transformers first writes a readable ABC-notation score (melody, chords, key,
meter, tempo, form), expands it into semantic music tokens, then realizes 48-kHz stereo
audio by flow matching. Trained on ~346k hours of music, whole songs packed in a 24,576
context. Two new tools supply supervision from raw recordings: MERT2 (music representation,
new SOTA on 14 of 15 MARBLE metrics) and SheetSage2 (lead-sheet transcription, leads 12 of
15 benchmark-metric pairs).

WHAT IT ACTUALLY SHOWED (their numbers)
- Same-checkpoint ablation: experts prefer symbolic planning over no planning, 49.3% vs
  34.6% overall (rest ties) - the planning step itself is doing measurable work.
- WildSongBench: 6.73 SongBench Global Avg, above all evaluated public baselines; best-of-8
  reaches 6.96, highest mean observed among evaluated systems.
- Expert listening: best-of-8 preferred over Suno v4.5; nearly balanced against Suno v5.
- Same checkpoint does score editing (edits change targeted melody/harmony while largely
  preserving the rest), zero-shot covers (beats both evaluated cover systems on all eight
  work-identity retrieval measures across 948 unseen works), and agentic editing via an
  external LLM rewriting the score.

LIMITS
- "Competitive with proprietary systems" is expert-preference based; it is not ahead of
  Suno v5 overall.
- Score adherence (does the audio follow the written notes exactly) is evaluated by
  agreement measures, not guaranteed; their editing claim says "largely preserving".
- Scale: 3.58B params, flow-matching decoder, trained on ~346k hours of music. I have not
  verified what training or fine-tuning it would cost; inference on a 16 GB card is plausible
  but not verified by me.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR KRAFTBEAT (my judgment)
- Your 18 Oct KraftBeat spec lists YuE2 integration (whether it is implemented yet, I have
  not verified); this paper is the capability ground truth for that decision. The important fact for the app: the editable interface is the ABC
  score, not the audio. KraftBeat's editing UX should be score-level (change chords/melody
  in text, re-render) - that is what the same-checkpoint editing mode supports.
- SheetSage2 is relevant to your audio-to-symbolic path, with a task caveat: it transcribes
  recordings to LEAD SHEETS (melody + chord symbols), while NeuralNote does polyphonic
  audio-to-MIDI transcription - overlapping but different outputs. It is not a drop-in
  NeuralNote replacement; evaluate it where a lead-sheet/chord-level representation is what
  KraftBeat actually needs, and keep NeuralNote for dense polyphonic MIDI.
- MERT2 is a CANDIDATE embedding for similarity/retrieval over a sample library: 14-of-15
  MARBLE metrics is a public-benchmark result, so measure it on your own library and
  retrieval task before committing.

2. StepAudio 3 Music Technical Report
arXiv:2609.16034 (11 Sep 2026), StepFun / CUHK / UCSD
Attention: 107 upvotes, #6 HF daily of 16 Sep
Coverage: full HTML text read (abstract, intro, system overview, ABC-CoT planning);
evaluation tables read at claim level.

WHAT IT IS
A long-form music model with the same big idea as YuE2, arrived at independently: an MoE
autoregressive model first writes an ABC-notation arrangement plan (ABC-CoT: chords, tempo,
meter, key, bar structure, melody), then predicts music tokens conditioned on it; a
flow-matching DiT renders 48-kHz audio. Their tokenizer finding: at 25 Hz, single-codebook
VQ gives more stable long-range musical development than multi-codebook RVQ even though RVQ
reconstructs better - "the representation that reconstructs best is not necessarily the one
best suited to autoregressive generation". They use a 50-Hz 65,536-entry single codebook.
Up to 5m30s songs; accompaniment from dry vocals; cover synthesis; DPO alignment.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY SHOWED (their numbers)
- Highest AudioBox Content Enjoyment / Content Usefulness / Production Quality and highest
  MuQ-MuLan similarity among evaluated systems; competitive SongBench.
- Preliminary Artificial Analysis Music Arena Vocals Elo 1105 - behind Suno V5.5 and Mureka,
  ahead of Suno V5 and MiniMax.
- They are explicit that note/chord-level adherence to the plan is an open empirical
  question, separate from caption alignment.

LIMITS
- A technical report, not a controlled study: the ABC-CoT benefit is asserted with system
  results, not isolated the way YuE2's same-checkpoint ablation isolates planning.
- No public weights mentioned in what I read; treat as design evidence, not a component.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR KRAFTBEAT (my judgment)
- Two independent frontier-quality systems in one month (this and YuE2) made the same
  design choice: a readable symbolic score as the planning interface. That is convergence
  evidence for the design among open systems - a factual observation, not a forecast.
- The tokenizer trade-off finding is the reusable lesson: when you pick representations for
  KraftBeat's own pipeline (or evaluate YuE2 vs alternatives), generation stability and
  reconstruction quality are different objectives; measure both.

3. TinyAudio: Compact and Efficient Text-to-Audio Generation for Low-Resource Deployment
arXiv:2609.31525 (25 Sep 2026), Liu et al., SJTU X-LANCE
Code: https://github.com/junxi25liu/TinyAudio
Coverage: PDF downloaded and read (method, deployment section, results tables).

WHAT IT IS
An 87M-parameter text-to-audio model (35M flow-matching DiT + 32M text encoder + 20M VAE
decoder), built for low-resource deployment. A MeanFlow variant (TinyAudio-MF) generates in
one function evaluation.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY SHOWED (PDF-verified, full model vs MF variant separated)
- Footprint (both variants): 87M total params, 0.48 GB peak GPU memory - over 90% smaller
  and over 8x less memory than every compared ~1B-param pipeline.
- Full TinyAudio: RTF 0.078 on their GPU setting (single GPU, FP32, batch size 1; vs
  0.257-4.173 for compared systems); AudioCaps FD 18.24, FAD 2.65 (vs best 14.06 / 2.07) -
  competitive but not best quality.
- TinyAudio-MF (MeanFlow one-step variant): RTF 0.010 on the same GPU setting, but quality
  drops to FD 23.65 in their own table. On CPU (container cgroup-limited to a four-core
  quota, FP32): steady-state RTF 0.715 for 10-second audio - faster than real time, but
  that is the MF variant with the quality drop, not the full model.
- Net: the full model is the quality/footprint story; MF is the speed story, and the two
  claims do not come from the same checkpoint.

LIMITS
- Text-to-AUDIO (sound scenes/effects), not music. This does not generate songs.
- CPU real-time claim rests on the one-step MF variant at reduced quality.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR KRAFTBEAT (my judgment)
- Not a music engine, but a candidate for a sound-design side feature (texture/foley/FX
  generation for beats): the reported 0.48 GB peak GPU memory is on-paper compatible with
  your 5060 Ti's VRAM - I have not verified it fits or runs on your machine, and their
  four-core CPU-quota result comes from their cgroup-limited container, so it does not
  establish performance on your CPU. If KraftBeat's scope is strictly music, skip it.
- The MeanFlow one-step trick is worth knowing as a sampling-acceleration pattern.
